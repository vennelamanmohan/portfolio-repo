04 · HABITNU · HEALTH TECH · 2023

From spreadsheets to a campaign tool that says what to do next

HabitNu helps people manage chronic disease through lifestyle change, in partnership with insurers and health providers. Before anyone is coached, they have to enrol. I designed the tool the marketing team used to run that outreach.

ROLE
Product designer (contract)
USERS
Marketing & enrolment team
CHANNELS
SMS, IVR calls, email
RESULT
50% faster setup · +23% onboarding

The problem

Outreach ran on scattered spreadsheets, so nobody could see which channel was actually enrolling people.

Slow setup
Every campaign meant juggling separate tools for contacts, content and scheduling.
No visibility
Conversion by channel was invisible while a campaign was running.
Budget on habit
Money kept going to underperforming channels because nothing showed otherwise.
DECISION 01

Six short steps, not one long form

A campaign touches different kinds of information, often owned by different people. I split setup into basic info, contacts, channel configuration, success metrics, budget and review, with drafts saving automatically, so a marketer waiting on a contact list could leave and come back without losing work.

Contacts already lived in Excel, so instead of forcing a new format the upload maps any sheet's columns to the right fields.

Early wireframe of the channel step: pick a channel, write the message, map replies to actions (reply "1" → join program), schedule.

DECISION 02

Triage from the list

The team ran many campaigns at once across partners. Every campaign got a plain health label (Good, Needs attention, At risk) next to its target pool and conversions, so the list itself answers "what do I look at first?" without opening each one.

DECISION 03

Say what the numbers mean

Charts alone left the team to do the analysis. Each campaign page opens with two plain-language panels: what the data shows ("SMS has the highest response rate at 39.7%") and what to do about it ("Reallocate 10% of email budget to SMS"). The charts underneath are there to check the reasoning, not to start from.

Outcome

Looking back

This was 2023. Today I'd treat the suggestions panel as the product, not a feature: test message variants automatically, and alert the team the moment a campaign slips to At risk instead of waiting for someone to open the list.
