03 · TRAVALOT · INTERNAL TOOLS · 2025

One screen that shows where bookings leak

Travalot's CRM could tell the team what stage every booking was in. It couldn't tell leadership whether the business was getting better at selling, who was carrying it, or which money was about to slip. I designed the dashboard that answers those three questions.

ROLE
Product designer (freelance), acting PM
SCOPE
Brief to PRD, IA, dashboard, drill-downs, visual design
USERS
Founder, sales leadership, team leads in four cities
DELIVERED
3 tabs, ~12 drill-downs, metric definitions

The brief

A Travalot booking changes hands many times: lead, quote, instalments, travel, review. The CRM let the team work inside one stage at a time, but nothing showed the whole picture — who's blocking a booking, who owns the next step, how much revenue is at risk. The founder framed it as five goals:

Speed Control Clarity Accountability Learning
THE TURN

"Are those 10 fulfilled trips from these 45 leads?"

My first concept gave each role its own dashboard, centred on deals per stage. Walking through it, I asked myself that question — and they weren't the same group. The 45 leads were this month's enquiries; the 10 trips were booked months earlier.

"22% conversion" compared two different groups and described no one. Conversion moved to period comparison, and the dashboard was reorganised around leadership's questions instead of job titles.

NO PM ON THE PROJECT

From brief to PRD

Before designing screens, I turned the brief into a one-page PRD the founder and developers could sign off.

PROBLEM STATEMENT
Leadership can't see, in one place, whether sales are improving, who's driving them, or which bookings and payments are slipping.

Agreeing what the numbers mean

I defined every metric before drawing a chart, so everyone would read a number the same way.

Conversion rateBookings won ÷ leads, in the same periodThe first concept mixed months and overstated it
Drop-off %Leads lost between two stages ÷ leads that enteredShows where to act, not just how many were lost
Days in stageAverage time a lead sits before moving onCatches stalls before they become drop-offs
Response timeLead created to first replyRevenue alone hides slow replies
Amount overdueInstalments past their due date, in rupeesTurns a count into a priority

What to build first, and why

PHASE 1
Operations + the shared drill-down template
Overdue follow-ups and payments lose money every day, and the template is reused by about a dozen pages.
PHASE 2
Overview + Pipeline trend
Needed the metric definitions agreed first; feeds the weekly leadership review.
PHASE 3
Team performance + reviews
Ranking people on unproven data damages trust, so it waited until phases 1 and 2 proved the numbers.

Trade-off: period comparison, not full cohort tracking, for the first release — it answers the question with data the CRM already held. Out of scope: role views, automated alerts, Leave management.

THE DESIGN

Each tab answers one question. Every number opens its list.

How are we doing this month?

Where we disagreed

The founder had the final say. I made my case, then designed his choice to work as well as it could.

I PROPOSED
Keep leave management out. It answers no sales question.
WE SHIPPED
A Leave tab. Leads check who's away before reassigning a lead.
HOW I MADE IT WORK
Last tab, outside the daily flow, so the sales tabs still read first.
I PROPOSED
Sorted bars for cancellations and reviews. Angles are slow to compare.
WE SHIPPED
Donut charts. He presents the Overview in leadership reviews.
HOW I MADE IT WORK
Fixed legend order, and every chart opens the full list with exact numbers.
I PROPOSED
Rank by conversion, visible to team leads only.
WE SHIPPED
A revenue leaderboard with trophies, for visible competition.
HOW I MADE IT WORK
Margin, conversion and response time sit beside revenue, so nobody reads it on revenue alone.

How success is measured

I handed over three tabs, a dozen drill-downs and the metric definitions, ready for development. Whether it works shows up in three numbers:

Amount overdue
Week on week. Operations exists to bring it down.
Quote-to-booking
Days in Quoted should fall first, where slow follow-up costs most.
Weekly review prep
From hours of clicking through stages to opening one tab.

What I took from it

Definitions are design. The metric table mattered more than any screen — my first concept looked right while measuring the wrong thing.

Organise by the question, not the org chart. Role dashboards duplicated the same data three ways; three questions gave each tab one job.

Next: a cohort view, overdue-follow-up alerts, and a trimmed Operations view as each trip expert's start screen.
