01 · SERVICENOW · AGENTIC AI · 2025–26

When to lead, when to step back

Designing an AI agent that schedules, books rooms for and reschedules interviews, across two shipped releases.

ROLE
Senior Product Designer, end to end
SCOPE
Conversation design, flows, human-in-the-loop, UI
USERS
Recruiters and coordinators
STATUS
Both releases shipped
THE SHORT VERSION

In the first release, recruiters knew the answer better than the agent, so it offered options and stepped aside. In the second, the agent knew more than any recruiter could hold in their head, so it led. An agent should lead in proportion to what it knows that the user doesn't.

The problem

Interview coordination is the slowest, most manual stage of hiring. Our design research found it drives about 65% of total time-to-hire.

40%
of interviews rescheduled at least once
30m–2h
recruiter time per reschedule cycle
~100h
recruiter capacity lost each month

Agentic scheduling was already live. Two gaps remained.

Rooms sat outside the agent. Recruiters booked rooms by hand and pasted details into the invite. Applicants received invites with no location.

Everything after the invite was manual. The moment anyone declined, the agent stopped and the recruiter started over. Scheduling was solved. Rescheduling wasn't.

RELEASE 1

Room booking: the recruiter knows best

01 · Get recruiters to their room, don't pick one for them

My first version picked a room and asked for confirmation. But recruiters schedule for the same hiring managers again and again, so they usually already know the room. A picked room is a step they'd override, so the agent offers three paths instead.

✦SCHEDULING AGENT
How would you like to find a meeting room?
I know which room Show me suggestions Skip room booking

Trade-off: one extra question up front, instead of a forced recommendation the recruiter has to undo.

02 · Only ask what it can't work out

Once a slot is chosen, the agent knows each interviewer's city and building. I mapped five location states and gave a question only to the ones that need it. Pick a state to see how the agent responds.

✦SCHEDULING AGENT
All the interviewers are in the same building, Argos. Here are rooms there that are free for the full hour.
NO QUESTION · SAYS WHAT IT INFERRED

When the agent infers something, it says so out loud, so bad room data is caught before the invite goes out.

03 · Room booking never holds the interview hostage

Skip is there at every step. Rejecting suggestions leads to search or manual entry, never a dead end, and skipping exits straight to the invite. Every branch ends in a sent invite, with or without a room.

04 · Open with context, not a question

A decline arrives unannounced, so the agent's first message carries everything needed to decide, with the most common path recommended.

✦SCHEDULING AGENT
Iris Martinez declined Alex Chen's Technical Interview. Still confirmed: Marcus Webb, Jordan Kim. Interview is in 4 days.
Reschedule · recommended Swap interviewer Drop interviewer Keep as is

05 · Reasons, not percentages

Every new time is confirmed by the recruiter. Calendars are unreliable, a confirmation costs seconds, and a wrongly booked panel costs four people's time. What makes it fast is the slot card: each one ends in a reason a person can act on.

Thu, 10:00 – 11:00BEST MATCH
All 4 participants free. No conflicts detected.
Thu, 17:00 – 18:00
All free. Late in the day; applicants prefer earlier.
Fri, 11:30 – 12:30LOW CONFIDENCE
Applicant availability unconfirmed.

06 · Show every side effect before acting

A reschedule rewrites other people's calendars, so one final confirmation lists every consequence: the cancellation, the removed event, the new invites to each named person. One tap, and the recruiter has seen everything the agent will do in their name.

07 · Options shrink honestly

When no slot works, the agent can widen the search once. If that fails too, the expand option disappears and the others stay. The recruiter never hits a dead end, and never sees an option that won't work.

08 · Edits reuse what already works

Changing the channel or swapping an interviewer doesn't get its own logic. Switching to in person hands off to the room booking conversation; swapping an interviewer ranks the hiring team with reasons, and flags the risk too.

Outcome

Both releases shipped
Room booking went out in the Zurich release; rescheduling and editing followed.
One pattern, whole lifecycle
Scheduling, rooms, reschedule on decline, no-slot recovery and edits.
Built once, reused
Room booking runs on its own and inside editing, with no second version.

What I'd do differently

Make accessibility a visible filter, not a free-text request. Skipping upfront preferences was right for speed, but it leaves accessibility to the recruiter's memory.

Measure how often recruiters override Best match. That number would tell us when the agent could safely book on its own, and move a checkpoint from the recruiter to the agent.
