A calculator that starts the coaching conversation

I turned a coaching practice that ran on Instagram and WhatsApp into a website with a free macros calculator at the front door. I scoped it, designed it and built it myself with AI-assisted code.

THE CALCULATOR

It looks like four steps and a number. Underneath, it reads a person's goal, lifestyle, diet and biggest struggle, and answers the way I would on a first call.

I share it on Instagram as a free resource, and many people find the coaching through their results.

The problem

People don't fail at nutrition for lack of information.

I kept hearing this in conversations with friends, people at the gym and people who reached out about coaching. So I ran a quick survey of about [34] people to test it. The hypothesis held: the barriers were time and perfectionism, not knowledge, and most had stopped tracking.

TESTED WITH PEOPLE FIRST
A 6-week WhatsApp pilot showed the group mattered more than the plan. That became 8-week coaching in circles of six, plus 1:1.
ONE HARD CONSTRAINT
Coaching is a passion project I run alongside design. My time goes to people who are genuinely ready to change.
PRODUCT DECISIONS

What I chose to build, and what I didn't

01 · The application is a commitment filter

A coach can guide an adult, but can't make them commit. So the application is long on purpose, and it asks why someone wants this now. The why tells me more than any other answer about whether they're serious.

Some people drop off because the form is long. I treat that as a signal, not a leak. It works like a good onboarding flow: the more someone puts in, the more personal the result feels and the more likely they are to follow through. Every 1:1 applicant gets a free discovery call, and anyone who isn't ready leaves with a few things to work on alone and an open door to come back.

02 · Useful answer first, email second

The email gate sits after the four steps, once someone has put in effort and wants their result. The full breakdown is emailed to them, which also starts the funnel for a newsletter I plan to run.

03 · Price as a filter, and as a swap

Group coaching is set at 40–50% of the 1:1 price, high enough that people who join intend to show up. The page answers "coaching is expensive" head-on: ₹6,400 over 8 weeks is about ₹115 a day, roughly a coffee. It isn't new money, it's moving a few small spends toward health.

04 · Copy that never goes stale

No button names a batch or a date. "Join the next circle" scrolls to the offer, and only "Apply" opens the form, so a missed date never breaks the page.

05 · The hero isn't about me

I tried my own photos first, then moved to a well-being wheel: food, movement, mindset and community. Much of my coaching is helping people define health on their own terms, so the page opens with their life.

THE LOGIC

Simple on the surface, a coach underneath

One protein number doesn't work for everyone — it depends on training, experience and body type. I wrote down how I read a new client on a first call and turned it into rules. Pick a layer.

LAYER 01
The baseline

A standard energy equation, adjusted for body type, how often and how hard someone trains, and how long they’ve been training.

The rules stay with me — they're what make two people with the same height and weight get different answers.

Couldn't someone get this from ChatGPT?

Partly, if they know which questions to ask, and most people don't. Even then, the answer stops at the numbers. Mine goes on to say what to work on first, based on what has worked for me and my clients.

VIBE-CODED

How I built it with AI

I designed the pages on the Claude canvas, then built them with Replit Agent: a React front end, a small Express server and a Postgres database. Results go out by email from my own domain.

The AI wrote most of the code. I owned everything that decides behaviour.

THE SPEC
Each rule written as plain if-this-then-that before any prompt, so the output could be checked against something.
THE EDGE CASES
Unrealistic goals, unusual heights, mixed diet styles. Tested by hand, failures fed back.
THE WORDS
Every result line written in my voice as a coach. Generated copy was the first thing I replaced.
ACCESSIBILITY
All motion switches off for people who turn on reduced motion.
AFTER LAUNCH

Listening, then cutting

I collected 27 pieces of feedback from friends and my own testing, and tagged each one: will do, explore, to decide, or not doing (with a reason). Five made the ship-first list.

The hardest note was the most useful. A designer friend said the calculator felt generic, like ChatGPT. When I asked why, he said ChatGPT's food suggestions never matched what he actually eats. Personalisation had to go further than the numbers.
